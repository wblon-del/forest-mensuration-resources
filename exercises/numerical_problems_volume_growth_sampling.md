# Numerical Problems: Volume, Growth and Sampling

## Problem 1 — Individual-tree volume
A tree has DBH 50 cm, total height 22 m and form factor 0.48. Estimate volume using `V = g × H × f`.

**Solution:**
`g = π(0.50²)/4 = 0.19635 m²`

`V = 0.19635 × 22 × 0.48 ≈ 2.07 m³`

**Answer: 2.07 m³.**

## Problem 2 — Stand volume
A 0.05-ha plot contains trees with estimated stem volumes totaling 9.5 m³. Estimate volume per hectare.

**Solution:** `9.5 / 0.05 = 190 m³/ha.`

## Problem 3 — Periodic annual increment
Stand volume increases from 180 to 225 m³/ha over 5 years.

`PAI = (225 − 180)/5 = 9 m³/ha/year.`

## Problem 4 — Mean annual increment
A stand has cumulative yield of 240 m³/ha at age 30.

`MAI = 240/30 = 8 m³/ha/year.`

## Problem 5 — Fixed-area sampling
A 0.04-ha plot contains 28 trees and 2.75 m² total basal area.

Trees/ha = `28/0.04 = 700 trees/ha`.

BA/ha = `2.75/0.04 = 68.75 m²/ha`.

## Problem 6 — Sampling intensity
A forest covers 2,000 ha and 20 ha are sampled using fixed-area plots.

`Sampling intensity = 20/2000 × 100 = 1%`.

## Problem 7 — Biomass scaling
A 0.05-ha plot has 5,500 kg estimated above-ground biomass.

`Biomass/ha = 5,500/0.05 = 110,000 kg/ha = 110 t/ha.`

## Problem 8 — Carbon estimate
If biomass is 110 t/ha and the approved methodology specifies a carbon fraction of 0.47:

`Carbon = 110 × 0.47 = 51.7 t C/ha.`

## Problem 9 — Diameter increment
A tree's DBH increases from 32.4 cm to 35.1 cm over 3 years.

`Periodic diameter increment = 35.1 − 32.4 = 2.7 cm.`

`Average annual increment = 2.7/3 = 0.9 cm/year.`

## Problem 10 — Quadratic mean diameter
Five trees have DBH values of 20, 25, 30, 35 and 40 cm.

`Dq = √[(20² + 25² + 30² + 35² + 40²)/5]`

`Dq = √950 ≈ 30.82 cm.`

**Answer: approximately 30.8 cm.**

> Instructor note: These problems are teaching examples. Operational volume and biomass estimates should use approved local equations and protocols.
