# Forest Mensuration — Lecturer Guide

## Teaching philosophy
Forest mensuration is best taught through a combination of quantitative reasoning and field observation. Students should understand not only how to calculate an answer but also whether the answer is reasonable and how measurement quality affects it.

## Before each lecture
- Review the learning outcomes.
- Prepare one local or realistic field example.
- Check that formulas use consistent units.
- Prepare a worked calculation before class.
- Identify one likely student misconception.

## During lectures
1. Introduce the field problem.
2. Define the variable precisely.
3. Explain the measurement procedure.
4. Derive or present the formula.
5. Work through a numerical example.
6. Ask students to estimate the expected magnitude.
7. Give a short problem for individual work.
8. Discuss sources of error and interpretation.

## Common misconceptions to address

- circumference is not diameter;
- DBH is a measurement convention, not simply any stem diameter;
- trees/ha and basal area/ha are different attributes;
- MAI is not the same as annual growth for every period;
- slope distance is not automatically horizontal distance;
- a volume equation is not universally valid for every species/site;
- sampling error and measurement/data-entry error are different; and
- biomass estimates depend on model choice and assumptions.

## Field teaching
Demonstrate a measurement first. Then let students measure independently. Compare results immediately and ask students to explain discrepancies before providing the answer.

## Data-quality teaching
Require students to preserve raw field data, document corrections, use consistent units and investigate outliers against field observations.

## Suggested Bhutan application
Use examples from plantations, community forests, forest management regimes and forest inventories. Where approved local volume or biomass equations exist, substitute them for generic teaching equations and explain why local calibration matters.

## Minimum student competency
Before completion, students should be able to measure DBH and height, calculate basal area and density, estimate volume using an approved method, design a basic sample inventory, calculate basic growth measures, and critically evaluate data quality.
