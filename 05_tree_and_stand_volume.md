# Module 5: Tree and Stand Volume

## Learning objectives

Students should understand the concept of stem volume and distinguish direct volume calculations from estimates based on measurements and form assumptions.

## 5.1 Basic cylinder approximation

A simple stem-volume approximation is:

**V = g × H**

where g is basal area and H is the relevant stem height.

Because real tree stems taper, a form adjustment is generally needed.

## 5.2 Form factor

A form factor can be expressed as:

**f = actual stem volume / (basal area × reference height)**.

Thus:

**V = g × H × f**.

The reference height and form-factor definition must be kept consistent.

### Example

DBH = 40 cm, height = 20 m and form factor = 0.45.

Basal area:

g = π(0.40²)/4 = 0.1257 m².

Volume:

V = 0.1257 × 20 × 0.45 ≈ **1.131 m³**.

## 5.3 Volume from sectional measurements

A stem can be divided into sections. Sectional volumes may be estimated using methods such as the Smalian, Huber or Newton formula, depending on the data available and the intended standard.

For a section with end areas A1 and A2 and length L, the Smalian formula is:

**V = L(A1 + A2)/2**.

## 5.4 Stand volume

Stand volume per hectare can be estimated by summing tree volumes in a sample plot and expanding to a hectare:

**V/ha = total sample-plot volume / plot area (ha)**.

## Important caution

Volume equations and local volume tables should be used only within their intended species, size, site and measurement ranges. Do not assume a generic form factor is valid for all species or stands.

## Exercise

A 0.05-ha plot contains trees with estimated volumes totaling 8.5 m³. Calculate volume per hectare.

**Answer:** 8.5 / 0.05 = **170 m³/ha**.
