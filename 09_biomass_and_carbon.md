# Module 9: Biomass and Carbon Estimation

## 9.1 Biomass

Biomass is the mass of living biological material. Forest biomass assessments commonly distinguish above-ground and below-ground components.

## 9.2 Measurement approaches

Biomass can be estimated using destructive sampling, biomass equations, biomass expansion factors or other validated approaches.

A common allometric form is:

**AGB = a × Dᵇ**

or, where height is included:

**AGB = a × Dᵇ × Hᶜ**

The coefficients must come from a validated equation appropriate to the species, forest type, units and range of measurements.

## 9.3 Carbon estimation

A simple carbon estimate is:

**Carbon = Biomass × carbon fraction**.

The carbon fraction should be based on the applicable standard or measured value rather than assumed without justification.

## 9.4 Example

If an approved equation estimates above-ground biomass at 250 kg for a tree and the adopted carbon fraction is 0.47:

Carbon = 250 × 0.47 = **117.5 kg C**.

## Important caution

Do not transfer an allometric equation between regions, species or diameter ranges without checking its validity. Equation choice can substantially affect biomass and carbon estimates.

## Practical exercise

Provide students with DBH and height measurements for 20 trees and an approved local/regional biomass equation. Have them calculate tree biomass, carbon stock and plot-level estimates.
