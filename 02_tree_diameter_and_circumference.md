# Module 2: Tree Diameter and Circumference

## Learning objectives

Students should be able to define DBH, measure circumference and diameter correctly, convert between them, and identify common sources of error.

## 2.1 Diameter at breast height (DBH)

DBH is the stem diameter measured at a standardized breast-height point. A commonly used convention is **1.3 m above ground**, but the applicable institutional or national standard should always be followed consistently.

## 2.2 Measuring circumference

Circumference can be measured using a diameter tape or flexible tape. The tape should be horizontal, snug around the stem, and free from loose branches or irregularities at the measurement point.

## 2.3 Converting circumference and diameter

The relationship between circumference (C) and diameter (D) is:

**D = C / π**

and:

**C = πD**

### Example

If C = 94.2 cm:

D = 94.2 / 3.1416 ≈ **30.0 cm**.

## 2.4 Special stem conditions

The standard measurement point may need to be adjusted for buttresses, leaning trees, forks, swelling, deformities or obstacles. The adjusted height and reason should be recorded according to the field protocol.

## 2.5 Instruments

- Diameter tape
- Measuring tape
- Calipers, where appropriate
- DBH pole or marked staff for locating the measurement point
- Field notebook or digital data-collection device

## 2.6 Common errors

- measuring above or below the defined point;
- holding the tape at an angle;
- allowing the tape to sag;
- measuring over vines or loose bark;
- recording the wrong unit; and
- rounding measurements inconsistently.

## Practical activity

Measure DBH or circumference of at least 20 trees. Record tree number, species, measurement height, diameter/circumference, special condition and observer initials. Repeat five measurements independently to assess consistency.

## Questions

1. A tree has a circumference of 125.6 cm. Calculate diameter.
2. Why must the measurement height be standardized?
3. How should an irregular or buttressed tree be treated under your institutional field protocol?

**Answer to Q1:** 125.6 / π ≈ **40.0 cm**.
