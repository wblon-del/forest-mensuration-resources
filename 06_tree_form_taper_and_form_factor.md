# Module 6: Tree Form, Taper and Form Factor

## 6.1 Taper

Tree taper is the decrease in stem diameter from the base toward the top. Taper affects stem form, merchantable volume and product recovery.

## 6.2 Tree form

Tree form describes how closely a stem resembles a geometric solid such as a cylinder, paraboloid, cone or neiloid. Real stems do not perfectly match one shape.

## 6.3 Form factor

Form factor summarizes stem form by comparing actual stem volume with a reference geometric volume. Different definitions use different reference diameters and heights, so the definition must always accompany the value.

## 6.4 Why form matters

Two trees with the same DBH and height can have different volumes because of differences in taper and stem shape.

## 6.5 Teaching demonstration

Ask students to compare three stems or stem models with similar basal diameter but different taper. Have them predict which has the greatest volume, then calculate sectional volumes.

## Discussion questions

1. How does strong taper affect merchantable volume?
2. Why is a form factor not universally transferable among species and sites?
3. What measurements would you collect to develop a local volume equation?

## Practical exercise

Measure diameters at several heights on a felled stem or suitable model. Plot diameter against height and discuss the resulting taper profile.
