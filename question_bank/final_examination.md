# Forest Mensuration — Final Examination

**Suggested duration:** 3 hours  
**Total:** 100 marks

## Section A — MCQs (20 marks)

Answer 20 multiple-choice questions selected from the repository question bank.

## Section B — Short-answer questions (20 marks)

Answer any five. **4 marks each.**

1. Explain the importance of DBH in forest inventory.
2. Describe two methods of tree-height measurement.
3. Explain stand basal area and trees/ha.
4. Distinguish MAI, CAI and PAI.
5. Explain tree taper and form factor.
6. Compare fixed-area and variable-radius sampling.
7. Explain sources of biomass-estimation uncertainty.

## Section C — Numerical problems (30 marks)

1. Calculate basal area for DBHs of 20, 30, 40 and 50 cm and calculate their combined basal area.
2. A 0.04-ha plot contains 25 trees with total basal area of 2.10 m². Estimate trees/ha and BA/ha.
3. A stand has 160 m³/ha at age 20 and 220 m³/ha at age 25. Calculate PAI and MAI at age 25.
4. Estimate tree volume for DBH 60 cm, height 25 m and form factor 0.45.
5. A 0.05-ha plot has 7,500 kg biomass. Estimate biomass/ha and carbon/ha if the approved exercise methodology specifies a carbon fraction of 0.47.

## Section D — Field inventory design (15 marks)

Prepare a sampling design for a hypothetical 1,000-ha forest. Include objectives, sampling design, plot configuration, proposed sampling intensity, variables, field equipment, crew roles and quality-control procedures.

## Section E — Interpretation and professional application (15 marks)

A forest stand has high tree density but relatively low basal area, with most trees in small diameter classes. Interpret the stand structure and discuss what additional measurements or information would be required before making a silvicultural recommendation.

## General marking principles

Credit correct reasoning and transparent calculations. Require units for numerical answers. Where more than one valid inventory design is possible, assess whether the student's design is logically consistent with the stated objective and terrain/forest conditions.
