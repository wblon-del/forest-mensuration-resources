# Module 10: Field Data Quality and Analysis

## 10.1 Data quality

Good mensuration data should be accurate, consistent, complete, traceable and appropriately documented.

## 10.2 Field quality-control checklist

- Confirm plot identification and coordinates.
- Confirm species names or codes.
- Check tree numbers for duplicates or omissions.
- Check measurement units.
- Check DBH and height values for impossible or suspicious observations.
- Record unusual stems and deviations from the standard protocol.
- Re-measure a subset of trees for quality control.
- Preserve the original field record before data cleaning.

## 10.3 Outliers

An unusual value is not automatically an error. A very large tree may be biologically real. Investigate the field record, measurement conditions and data-entry process before deleting or changing an observation.

## 10.4 Basic descriptive statistics

For a set of measurements, useful summaries include:

- number of observations;
- minimum and maximum;
- mean;
- median;
- standard deviation; and
- coefficient of variation.

## 10.5 Data workflow

**Field measurement → recording → verification → data entry → quality control → calculation → analysis → interpretation → reporting.**

## Practical exercise

Give students a small tree inventory dataset containing deliberate unit errors, duplicate tree numbers and one genuine large tree. Ask them to identify issues, document corrections and calculate summary statistics without automatically deleting the genuine observation.

## Key lesson

A mathematically correct calculation based on poor field data still produces a poor estimate. Measurement quality and data management are fundamental parts of forest mensuration.
