# Module 4: Basal Area and Stand Density

## 4.1 Basal area

Basal area is the cross-sectional area of a tree stem at a specified reference height, commonly DBH.

For a circular stem:

**g = πD² / 4**

where D is diameter in metres and g is basal area in m².

An equivalent formula when DBH is in centimetres is:

**g = πD² / 40,000**

### Example

For DBH = 30 cm:

g = π(30²)/40,000 = **0.0707 m²/tree**.

## 4.2 Basal area per hectare

For a sample plot:

**BA/ha = total basal area measured in plot / plot area in ha**.

Example: if 2.4 m² of basal area is recorded in a 0.05-ha plot:

BA/ha = 2.4 / 0.05 = **48 m²/ha**.

## 4.3 Stand density

Stand density is commonly expressed as the number of trees per hectare.

**Trees/ha = number of trees counted / plot area in hectares**.

Example: 35 trees in 0.05 ha gives **700 trees/ha**.

## 4.4 Importance

Basal area and density help describe stand structure, compare sites, support stocking assessments, and inform silvicultural decisions.

## Practical exercise

In a 0.04-ha plot, students record 28 trees with a combined basal area of 1.96 m².

Calculate:

1. trees/ha;
2. basal area/ha.

**Answers:** 700 trees/ha and 49 m²/ha.

## Field note

When using angle-count or variable-radius sampling, the relationship between inclusion probability and basal area must be understood before converting observations to stand estimates.
