# Module 1: Introduction to Forest Mensuration

## 1.1 Definition

**Forest mensuration** is the science and practice of measuring trees and forest stands and using those measurements to describe, compare, manage and monitor forest resources.

It combines field measurement, mathematics, statistics and interpretation.

## 1.2 Why forest mensuration matters

Forest managers need reliable measurements to answer questions such as:

- How large are the trees?
- How many trees occur per hectare?
- How much basal area is present?
- How much timber volume is available?
- How fast is the forest growing?
- How much biomass or carbon is stored?
- Has a forest management intervention changed stand structure?

## 1.3 Levels of measurement

### Tree level

Common variables include diameter, circumference, total height, merchantable height, crown dimensions and stem form.

### Stand level

Common variables include trees per hectare, basal area per hectare, volume per hectare, stocking, diameter distribution and growth.

### Forest or landscape level

Measurements are aggregated through inventories, sampling and spatial analysis.

## 1.4 Basic measurement principles

Good mensuration requires:

1. a clearly defined measurement objective;
2. standardized measurement points and procedures;
3. appropriate instruments;
4. representative sampling;
5. accurate recording with units;
6. repeatable methods; and
7. quality control.

## 1.5 Common units

- Diameter: cm or m
- Circumference: cm or m
- Height: m
- Basal area: m²/tree or m²/ha
- Volume: m³/tree or m³/ha
- Density: trees/ha
- Growth: cm/year, m/year or m³/ha/year

## 1.6 Key concept

A measurement is useful only when the **variable, unit, method and measurement point** are clearly defined.

**Answer:** 25 / 0.05 = **500 trees/ha**.
